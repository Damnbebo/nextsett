# Homelessness Presentation Notes

## Problem Definition, Community Impact, Evidence
- 650,000+ people were homeless on a single night in Jan 2023, a 12% YoY increase (HUD AHAR 2023).
- Rent has outpaced wages; a worker needs $28.58/hr for a modest 2-bedroom at fair market rent—nearly 2x the federal minimum wage (NLIHC Out of Reach 2023).
- NJ's 2024 Point-in-Time count found 12,000+ residents unhoused in one night; waitlists for vouchers and SRAP stretch years.
- Black residents are 13% of the US population but 37% of the unhoused, reflecting structural inequities (NAEH 2024).
- Community impacts include higher emergency service spending, healthcare costs, policing, and intergenerational poverty; children experiencing homelessness face lower educational attainment and adult earnings.

## Key Actors & Roles
- **Federal HUD + USICH**: fund Continuum of Care, Housing First pilots, AHAR data.
- **NAEH, NLIHC**: advocacy & research framing homelessness as affordability + equity crisis.
- **NJ Office of Homelessness Prevention (2019)**: coordinates statewide strategy, aligns grants.
- **HPRP, SRAP, Section 8**: short-term rental help and long-term vouchers; limited slots create bottlenecks.
- **Mental Health in Shelters law (A-4755)**: enables on-site behavioral health supports.
- **CEDD + DASH**: eviction defense and document assistance to keep tenants housed.
- **Municipal trust fund proposal (A3360)**: would let towns raise dedicated revenue for housing, not yet enacted.
- **Local governments, nonprofits, healthcare systems**: implement shelters, outreach, supportive services; face funding volatility, staff burnout, and coordination gaps.

## Policy Context & Institutional Constraints
- **McKinney-Vento + HEARTH**: establish federal commitment to emergency response + coordinated entry but remain underfunded.
- **Housing First**: evidence-based but capacity-limited; long waitlists erode impact.
- **Section 8, LIHTC, SRAP**: central affordability levers constrained by budget caps.
- **Local ordinances** (anti-camping, sit-lie bans): displace people, create criminal records, and complicate reentry.
- **Institutional barriers**: fragmented data systems, complex paperwork, Medicaid billing rules, political turnover, and uneven municipal priorities hamper continuity of care.

## Challenges
- Homelessness is a "wicked problem" intersecting labor markets, healthcare, criminal justice, and housing supply.
- Chronic shortage of deeply affordable units; production lags far behind demand.
- Short-term assistance cycles people in/out of shelters; shelters may feel unsafe or overly rigid.
- Deinstitutionalization without community investments, plus NIMBYism and criminalization, created unintended harms.
- Data gaps from hidden homelessness (couch surfing, vehicles) impede proportional responses.

## Proposed Solutions
- Scale Housing First with wraparound care; pair keys with case management, mental health, addiction treatment.
- Expand affordable housing pathways (public housing revival, rent-capped units, vouchers, local trust funds).
- Strengthen prevention: living wages, eviction defense, flexible cash aid, reentry planning from hospitals/jails.
- Invest in behavioral health workforce, mobile clinics, and trauma-informed shelter standards.
- Decriminalize survival behaviors; shift to outreach-led responses and community partnerships.
- Build integrated data/coordination hubs so state, county, nonprofit, and health actors share accountability.
